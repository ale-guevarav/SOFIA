\LabDSTcomment{R4.2}{A}{Section~\ref{sec:intro}}
{No hay planteamiento formal del problema, pregunta de investigación ni objetivos.}
{We added a formal Problem Statement, Research Question, and Objectives in natural narrative format at the end of the Introduction (Section~\ref{sec:intro}), addressing per-servo time-constant estimation and actuation synchronization. The revised text now reads:}
\LabDSTquote{Standard low-cost micro-servos exhibit manufacturing tolerances that result in non-uniform actuation speeds and transient response times across different units. In a 16-unit, 32-actuator modular kinetic surface like SOFIA, driving all servos with uniform control signals creates dynamic motion desynchronization and mechanical stress during normal use.
To address this limitation, this work investigates the following research question: Can experimental estimation of individual servo mechanical time constants ($\tau_m$) and their integration into a trajectory controller improve dynamic positional synchronization across a 32-actuator kinetic surface?
This encompasses three primary objectives: (1) designing a PCB integrating an ESP32-C3 microcontroller, dual PCA9685 drivers, and RS-485 interfaces; (2) developing an automated micro-test protocol to capture step-response and extract individual time constants; and (3) integrating individual compensation parameters into the surface tracking controller to equalize dynamic settling times across all actuators.}
